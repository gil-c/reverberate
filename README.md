# reverberate

## The goal

`reverberate` explores a simple question: can we predict what a room sounds
like, by looking at its shape and the materials it is made of, without
running a full physical simulation?

The project builds a machine learning model that estimates the acoustic
response of a room (how sound reverberates, decays, and gets coloured inside
it) directly from its 3D geometry and the absorption properties of its
surfaces. The goal is a web app where users can upload a room,
see it rendered with its materials, and listen to a comparison of a sound
simulated physically versus the same sound generated by the learned model,
side by side.

## What is covered

- Generating a dataset of simulated rooms, built from furnished and labelled
  3D scenes, with automatic assignment of acoustic materials.
- A learned model that predicts how sound decays in a room, frequency band by
  frequency band, rather than a raw sound waveform.
- A demo application to visualise a room and listen to the result, simulation
  versus prediction.

## How it works

A room is represented by its 3D mesh and by the acoustic properties of each of
its surfaces (walls, floor, furniture). A reference simulator computes the
"true" acoustic response of that room to train the model. The model then
learns to recover, directly from the shape and materials, how sound decays
over time in that room, without repeating the simulation each time. Once
trained, it allows quickly testing many variants of a room (materials,
layout) where simulation alone would be too slow to do so.
